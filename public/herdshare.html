How it works

Why it is a herd share, not a milk sale

Ohio does not allow farms to sell raw milk. It does allow you to own a dairy animal and drink the milk from it. A herd share is exactly that: you legally own part of the herd, the farm boards and milks your share, and the milk from your own animal comes to you.

  1. Buy your share

    A one-time $25 share makes you a part-owner of the herd. It is refundable if you ever withdraw from the contract, because the farm buys your share back.

  2. Pay weekly board

    $14 a week pays D&L Meadows to house, feed, care for, and milk your share of the herd.

  3. Pick up your milk here

    The farm delivers herd share milk to The Wellness Way – Mason every Friday morning around 9 AM. It goes in a cooler out front, so you can grab yours any time Friday, even after the office closes at noon.

Where we fit in. The Wellness Way – Mason does not sell milk, does not take a cut, and is not part of your contract. We are simply a convenient pickup spot on the farm's route so patients can get clean, local raw milk without driving to Clermont County.

Why goat milk

Richer, gentler, and closer to the source

Nigerian Dwarf goats give milk with a higher butterfat than most dairy breeds, which is why herd share members describe it as rich and creamy with no "goaty" aftertaste. Many people who struggle with cow's milk find goat milk easier on their digestion. Its fat globules are smaller, its protein structure is different, and it is naturally lower in the A1 casein that some people react to.

Raw means it is not pasteurized or homogenized. The enzymes, beneficial bacteria, and fat-soluble vitamins arrive the way the goat made them. If you would rather not drink it raw, you can gently pasteurize it at home on the stove and still come out well ahead of store prices.

  • Milked on a Grade A licensed dairy with a closed milk line, not by hand, so nothing falls into the milk
  • Chilled fast right after milking, then delivered cold
  • Goats are registered with the American Dairy Goat Association and the American Goat Society, and the herd is on a monthly milk-testing program
  • Use it like whole milk: drink it, cook with it, make kefir or yogurt, or feed it to the kids

An honest note on raw milk. Raw milk is not pasteurized, so it carries a small risk of foodborne illness that pasteurized milk does not. Public health agencies advise extra caution for pregnant women, infants and young children, older adults, and anyone with a weakened immune system. Read the farm's practices, ask your questions, and make the call that is right for your household. We are happy to talk it through at your next visit.

About the farm

Meet D&L Meadows

A small, family-owned Grade A goat dairy in Williamsburg, Ohio, run by the Smits family. It started as a 4-H style hobby and grew, one cute kid at a time, into a licensed dairy that now sends every drop of its milk to herd share families like yours.

The farm keeps Nigerian Dwarf goats and Mini Nubians alongside sheep, chickens, and rabbits. The Smits chose Nigerian Dwarfs because they are small, gentle, and still a true dairy breed. Several of their does hold official milking records, including one that gave more than 1,400 pounds of milk in a single 305-day lactation. The two Smits daughters handle much of the daily operation, from kidding and feeding to milking and animal care.

  • 2004 Two Nigerian Dwarf goats arrive as pets for the children. The offspring were too cute to sell.
  • 2012 The family starts milking for their own table.
  • 2015 Grade B dairy license. Milk goes to a local artisan cheese and yogurt maker.
  • 2017 Upgrade to a Grade A milk house and parlor. The herd share program begins.
  • 2019 A 4,000-square-foot hoop barn goes up for the growing herd.
  • 2020 A milk line is installed. Six goats can be milked at once.
  • Today Every gallon goes to herd share members across the Cincinnati area, including our office in Mason.

Visitors leave the farm talking about how clean it is, how well the animals are cared for, and how patiently the family answers every question. Farm tours run by appointment Monday through Saturday, about an hour long, for $10 a person. Kids get to bottle-feed a baby goat.
