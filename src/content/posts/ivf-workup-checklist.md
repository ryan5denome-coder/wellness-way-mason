Before you spend a great deal of money, time, and hope on a round of IVF, it's fair to ask one question: has anyone actually looked, carefully, at both of you?

A single IVF cycle in the United States can cost $15,000 to $25,000, according to a 2025 committee opinion from the American Society for Reproductive Medicine (ASRM) ([ASRM, Improving access to care, 2025](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/improving-access-to-care-and-delivery-to-marginalized-and-vulnerable-populations-a-committee-opinion-2025/)). That number is not a reason to skip IVF if it's the right next step with your fertility team. It is a reason to finish a thoughtful workup before you sign, or to keep asking good questions alongside the process if you have already started.

This checklist is meant to support a conversation with your OB, reproductive endocrinologist (REI), or urologist. It does not replace fertility care. ## You are not broken

The Wellness Way's article [You Are Not Broken: There's Hope After Infertility](https://www.thewellnessway.com/you-are-not-broken/) starts in a place we agree with completely: a hard time conceiving does not mean you are a broken person. The Wellness Way describes the body as a Swiss watch, with each part affecting the others. When a couple is trying to grow a family, they write, you are trying to get two Swiss watches to work together. That takes a careful look at both people, not a conclusion that "unexplained" means there is nothing left to see.

"Unexplained" infertility often means the testing has been incomplete. We hear versions of the same pattern: she has had the ultrasounds, the hormone panels, and a stack of appointments. He has had a cup and a clipboard.

ASRM's guidance on fertility evaluation says the workup should include ovulatory status, the structure and patency of the female reproductive tract, and a semen evaluation of the male partner, and that the male partner should be evaluated in parallel when it applies ([ASRM, Fertility evaluation of infertile women, 2021](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/fertility-evaluation-of-infertile-women-a-committee-opinion-2021/)). The American Urological Association (AUA) and ASRM say both partners should be assessed at the same time, and that the first look at him should include a reproductive history and one or more semen analyses ([AUA/ASRM guideline, 2020](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/diagnosis-and-treatment-of-infertility-in-men-auaasrm-guideline-part-i-2020/)).

Imaging, tubal studies, and the IVF plan itself belong with your fertility team. The list below is about labs and questions that are easy to assume were already done, looked at through a Wellness Way lens: test, don't guess, and look at what may be stressing the body.

## A checklist you can bring to your next appointment

Use this as a set of questions, not as a protocol we're telling you to demand.

**For her**

- **Thyroid, including antibodies.** A TSH is a start. ASRM notes that if TSH is abnormal, free T4 and thyroid autoantibodies are warranted ([ASRM, 2021](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/fertility-evaluation-of-infertile-women-a-committee-opinion-2021/)). A fair question is whether antibodies were included, especially if TSH has been off or if there have been losses.
- **Iron stores, not just a hemoglobin.** Ferritin reflects stored iron, which is a different question than whether you are anemic today.
- **Blood sugar over time.** A single fasting glucose doesn't always tell the whole story. Ask what has been looked at over time, and whether insulin or a hemoglobin A1c was part of the picture.
- **Hormones timed to her cycle.** The day of the blood draw matters. The Wellness Way often talks about looking at how hormones are made, converted, and cleared, not only whether one number was "in range." If your cycle is irregular, ask how they timed the labs. If your periods are painful or hard to predict, that is information too.
- **The structural workup.** Has anyone already looked at ovulation, the uterus, and the fallopian tubes in the way your REI recommends? That's their part of the map.

**For him**

- **A semen analysis is a start, not the whole picture.** AUA/ASRM recommend one or more semen analyses as part of the first evaluation, and they also note that a number above or below a cutoff does not, by itself, predict fertility or infertility ([AUA/ASRM, 2020](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/diagnosis-and-treatment-of-infertility-in-men-auaasrm-guideline-part-i-2020/)). If the first result is off, they recommend a second sample, ideally at least a month later.
- **His history and his health.** The same guideline starts with a reproductive history, not only a lab cup.
- **A real evaluation if something is off, or after hard losses.** AUA/ASRM say that men with abnormal semen parameters should be seen by a male reproductive expert, and that evaluation of the male should be considered after failed IVF cycles or two or more pregnancy losses ([AUA/ASRM, 2020](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/diagnosis-and-treatment-of-infertility-in-men-auaasrm-guideline-part-i-2020/)).

## Male factor and dads' health before conception

It takes two. After losses, or after a long stretch of trying, both people in the house deserve a look.

The Wellness Way's article [Men's Fertility](https://www.thewellnessway.com/mens-fertility/) makes that case directly. Citing the NIH, they note that in about one-third of infertile couples the difficulty is on the man's side, in about one-third it is on the woman's side, and in about one-third it involves both partners or isn't clearly identified. Their point is the same as ours: his health is not a side conversation.

That article also looks at testosterone, hidden inflammation, sugar, food reactions, and everyday toxins as part of the male fertility picture, not as a reason to blame him. The Wellness Way "You Are Not Broken" article adds that stress often has a large influence on a woman's hormones, while sugar is one of the first lifestyle pieces they ask men to look at. Habits can be a meaningful start. They are not the same thing as information. Testing is what tells you where he actually stands.

Our role, if you want us involved, is to help look at the broader health picture and to coordinate, not to take over fertility care. We don't use this blog to give medication advice, and we don't claim that changing one habit will change the outcome of a pregnancy. We do think he deserves the same seriousness she has already been given.

## How to use this without turning it into a fight

If a deposit is already on the calendar, you don't have to blow up the plan. You can say, "Before we take the next step, I want to make sure we've looked at these items for both of us." Bring the list. Ask what has already been done, and what still makes sense. If IVF is already underway, the same questions can still help.
