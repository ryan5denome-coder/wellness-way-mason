The week after Thanksgiving can feel like the start of a long run into December. If you live in Mason, Ohio, or anywhere around Cincinnati, you can feel the season speeding up. School programs, church gatherings, work parties, and family dinners still land in the same few weeks. The extra time with people you love is a gift. It can also leave you stretched thin, short on sleep, and a little less like yourself.

You don't have to overhaul your whole December to get through it. At The Wellness Way Mason, we hear this same squeeze every year at this time. The Wellness Way looks at stress as more than a mood. In [The Ultimate Stress Management Guide](https://www.thewellnessway.com/the-ultimate-stress-management-guide-restoring-balance-the-wellness-way/), they describe stress as a physiological event: cortisol and adrenaline rise, blood sugar can rise, and digestion and rest can take a back seat. A little of that is part of a full life. When it never turns off, sleep, gut comfort, and hormones can all start to feel the load.

The most useful help is usually simple: a short walk after a meal, a little margin between commitments, steadier food, and one quiet minute at the end of the day. Pick one or two of the practices below and let them be enough.

## Stress has a name at The Wellness Way: the 3 Ts

The Wellness Way approach starts with what chiropractic has long called the 3 Ts, laid out in ["The 3 T's": The Trauma, Toxins, and Thoughts That Stress The Body and Cause Dis-ease](https://www.thewellnessway.com/the-3-ts-the-trauma-toxins-and-thoughts-that-stress-the-body-and-cause-dis-ease/):

- **Traumas** are physical stressors: poor sleep, too much sitting, old injuries, a tense neck and shoulders, a packed calendar that never lets your body come down.
- **Toxins** are chemical stressors: sugar, processed holiday foods, foods you don't tolerate well, and the other everyday exposures that add up when the season gets loud.
- **Thoughts** are mental and emotional stressors: the to-do list, family dynamics, money worries, and the feeling that you should be enjoying all of this more than you are.

A packed calendar is often a sign that your life is full of people who matter to you. The hard part is that travel, later nights, richer meals, and a longer to-do list all arrive at once. Your body notices that stack. That does not mean you failed. It means the 3 Ts are all in the room at the same time.

## Take a short walk after you eat

Big holiday meals can send blood sugar up quickly and then drop it just as fast. That's often when you feel sluggish and start looking for something sweet. The Wellness Way stress guide also notes that the stress response itself can raise blood sugar, which is one more reason a long sit on the couch after dinner isn't always the kindest next step.

You don't need a workout plan for this. A [2022 systematic review in the journal Sports Medicine](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01649-4) found that short bouts of light walking, often just 2 to 5 minutes, were linked with a smaller rise in blood sugar after eating, compared with staying seated. If you have a little more time, a longer easy loop around the block is a comfortable next step.

After a big holiday meal, make it a family habit and walk before dessert. On cold or rainy Mason evenings, a few trips up the stairs or a couple of laps around the house still count. The point is to stand up, move a little, and let the meal settle.

## Leave a little margin, and keep one meal simple

When you can, leave 10 or 15 minutes between things. Step outside. Sit in the car for a moment before you walk into the next house. Make a slow cup of tea and actually drink it while it's still warm.

A small pause keeps the day from stacking into one long blur. It also gives your nervous system a chance to catch up. The Wellness Way stress guide includes simple nervous-system supports like slow breathing, a short walk, prayer or journaling, and a slightly earlier bedtime. You won't get a margin on every day in December. That's all right. Aim for one pause on the days you can see coming.

Not every dish has to be a project. A tray of roasted vegetables or a pot of soup can still feel like a feast, and it gives you back an hour. If you want a starting place, we already shared two simple fall recipes on the blog: [rosemary roasted root vegetables and cinnamon baked apples](/blog/thanksgiving-gut-friendly-recipes-mason-oh). Keeping one meal simple isn't about being strict. It's about lowering the food stress (the "Toxins" part of the 3 Ts) just enough that the week doesn't run only on leftover sweets and adrenaline.

## End the day with one quiet minute

Before the house goes dark, take a minute to name one thing you are grateful for. If prayer is part of your life, this is a natural place for it. If it isn't, a quiet breath and a simple thank-you still count.

The Wellness Way 3 Ts article names gratitude, love, and a quieter mind as part of how people work with mental stress. This doesn't have to be a long journal practice or a perfect routine. One honest sentence is enough. "I'm glad we made it around the table." "I'm thankful for a ride home." "I'm grateful the kitchen is finally clean." Say it, then go to bed.
