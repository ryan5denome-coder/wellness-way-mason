One of the pleasures of living in Mason, Ohio, is how close good food can be. You don't always have to drive across the city or guess at a label. Sometimes the farm or the bakery will meet you halfway, and in two cases this season, halfway is our office.

The Wellness Way Mason is a drop-off point for a goat dairy herdshare from D&L Meadows, a family-owned Grade A goat dairy in Williamsburg, Ohio, east of Cincinnati. We're also helping Behemoth Bakery, a gluten-free bakery in Fort Thomas, Kentucky, gather sign-ups for a weekly bread subscription with pickup here in the office.

Here's how each one works, what it costs as of the details we were given, and how to start. These aren't care plans, and they aren't a requirement of being a patient here. They're local options, shared so you can decide for yourself, especially if you already know that food quality and food reactions matter to you.

## Why we share local food finds

We're a chiropractic and functional health clinic, not a grocery store. We still care about what people put on the table, because real food is part of how many families feel during a long winter. The Wellness Way's holiday trainings come back to the same idea: start with real, whole foods, and be thoughtful about the foods that commonly stress the gut, including conventional dairy and gluten-containing grains for some people. When a nearby farm or bakery can make pickup easy for Mason-area families, we're glad to pass that along.

The Wellness Way also teaches that not every food is a fit for every person. That's why we test rather than guess when food reactions are part of the picture. A local loaf or a Friday milk drop can be a gift for one household and a poor fit for another. Use what you already know about your body, and ask questions before you join either program.

## D&L Meadows: a goat dairy herdshare east of Cincinnati

[D&L Meadows](https://dandlmeadows.com/products.htm) is a small, family-owned Grade A goat dairy in Williamsburg, Ohio. Through their herdshare, you purchase a share of the herd, they board and milk the goats, and you receive the milk and milk products from your share. If you leave the program, they buy the share back.

As listed on [their products page](https://dandlmeadows.com/products.htm): a share is $25, which is refundable when you withdraw, plus $14 per week for care and board. That weekly fee covers $14 worth of milk or milk products each week. If a full share is too much, a half share is also available, for a half gallon per week.

Their products include raw goat milk and farm-made cheeses. Their site notes that you can pasteurize the milk yourself if you prefer. Raw milk is not a fit for every household, so read the farm's information carefully and ask questions before you join.

The Wellness Way's article [Is Milk Good for You?](https://www.thewellnessway.com/is-milk-healthy/) is cautious about conventional cow's milk, and it notes that dairy is not automatically a good fit for everyone. Many people in Wellness Way clinics find they react to cow's milk, and some also react to goat's milk. That same article mentions goat milk as one option some people consider, along with other alternatives, and it encourages people to pay attention to how they feel. A local herdshare does not change that. If dairy has been a question for you, this is a conversation to have with us or with your own practitioner, not a food you have to add just because it is local.

## Behemoth Bakery: gluten-free bread, picked up here

Behemoth Bakery is a Fort Thomas, Kentucky bakery, and everything they make is gluten free, from whole grains and clean ingredients. You may have met them at our Knead to Know tasting in early November. If you didn't, you can still follow their work on Instagram at [@behemoth_bakery](https://www.instagram.com/behemoth_bakery/).

The Wellness Way's article [Is a Gluten-free Diet Healthy?](https://www.thewellnessway.com/is-a-gluten-free-diet-healthy/) makes an important distinction we agree with: a gluten-free label is not automatically a healthier choice if the food is still highly processed. What you replace gluten with matters. Behemoth's loaves are a whole-food style option, and office pickup means you don't have to drive to Northern Kentucky every week. Even then, gluten-free bread is meant for anyone who wants it, including people who already know wheat or gluten is a stressor for them, and it is still worth choosing in a way that fits your own body.

They're launching a weekly bread subscription, and they would like 10 sign-ups for pickup at The Wellness Way Mason. The price they want people to plan around is the monthly number. Plans start at $128 per month, and that monthly price includes shipping.

Here is the subscription as it was shared with us in November 2026. Please confirm current details when you reach out.

- **Plan B:** 2 loaves + 2 sweets, $216 per month ($44 per week)

Monthly plan prices include shipping. Loaves and sweets rotate.
