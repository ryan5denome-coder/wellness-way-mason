# PCOS page — source synthesis and editorial decisions

31 articles read, one agent each. 6 CORE, 7 ADJACENT, 18 MARGINAL/none.

## Load-bearing sources
- `pcos-natural-solutions` — the 3 T's applied to PCOS, fireman/carpenter, named test panels, 12-supplement list
- `symptoms-of-pcos` — the "9 silent symptoms", dismissal statistics, myth-busts
- `pcos-belly-fat-6-tips` — WHO prevalence, "PCOS belly" anatomy, 3 T's → HPA → insulin chain, AMPK herbs
- `adp-pcos-breast-cancer` — production vs conversion framing, "your doctor isn't lying to you" reframe
- `estrogen-dominance` / `metabolic-syndrome` / `endometriosis` — same template, gut-estrogen chain, No Sugar Panel
- `adp-statins-type2` — the six-step insulin-resistance loop, names PCOS at the end of it

## STATISTIC CONFLICT — must resolve before publishing
Two different prevalence figures appear across the library:
- **Up to 13%** of reproductive-age women, attributed to WHO (`pcos-belly-fat-6-tips`, cited)
- **10–21%** of reproductive-age women (`symptoms-of-pcos`, cited to refs [1][2])
- **1 in 10 women worldwide** (`pcos-natural-solutions`, UNCITED)
Decision: use the WHO figure only. It is the most defensible and the most conservatively stated.
The 70%-undiagnosed figure is also WHO-attributed and is the stronger hook anyway.

The 98% "doctors didn't take them seriously" figure is the single best emotional stat in the
library, but it traces to "2023 studies" described as recent in a Feb-2024 article. **Ryan must
verify the primary source before this goes live.** It is currently marked as needing sign-off.

## What went ON the page
- Dismissal-first opening (the emotional spine every agent identified)
- Silent/atypical symptoms — including the two myth-busts (no cysts required; normal testosterone
  possible) and lean PCOS at normal BMI, which is a genuinely underserved segment
- "PCOS belly" split into subcutaneous / visceral / bloating — the most differentiated single idea
  in the entire library
- The 3 T's, applied to PCOS specifically
- Production / conversion / elimination as the testing rationale
- Named panels only — no interpretation promises
- Fireman vs carpenter (critiques a toolkit, not a person — the safest brand metaphor available)
- An explicit "what we don't do" section

## What was DELIBERATELY LEFT OFF, and why
Every agent independently flagged the same categories. All excluded:

1. **The entire supplement protocol.** The parent brand names 12+ botanicals (inositol, NAC,
   spearmint, licorice, vitex, berberine, DIM, etc.) for PCOS by name. Recommending named
   botanicals for a diagnosed endocrine disorder is outside Ohio DC scope, and several of the
   parent brand's own citations are rodent studies presented in the same confident register as
   human RCTs (aloe = letrozole rats; vitex = male mice; ashwagandha = rats).
2. **Metformin and birth-control critique.** Present in at least six articles. A DC commenting on
   the appropriateness of a patient's prescription is scope overreach, and it is the single most
   repeated flag across all 31 reports.
3. **All cancer content.** The parent brand links PCOS to endometrial/breast cancer risk and, in
   places, implies dietary prevention. Not repeatable by a DC.
4. **"Chiropractic care supports hormone levels."** UNCITED in every article that makes it, and it
   is the claim most specific to the clinic's own service — i.e. the one most likely to draw a
   board complaint. Chiropractic appears on our page as musculoskeletal care only.
5. **Cure / reverse / overcome / restore-fertility language.** Pervasive upstream. Nothing on our
   page promises an outcome.
6. **"Root cause" branding** — banned per the clinic's own voice rules.

## Net effect
The Mason page is materially more conservative than the parent brand's PCOS content. It keeps the
brand's frameworks and its emotional argument, and drops the protocol and the drug commentary.
That is a deliberate trade: less to say, much less to defend.
