# Mono / Epstein Barr page: source synthesis and editorial decisions

Ten agents. One on Dr. DeNome's Instagram caption, nine on the articles returned by
`thewellnessway.com/?s=epstein+barr`.

## The finding that shaped the page

**There is no Epstein Barr article on thewellnessway.com.** Sixteen results reported, fourteen
real URLs, nine of them actual articles. EBV appears in each only in passing, between one and
four mentions, always as a single bullet in a list of triggers. `/docs/epstein-barr/` and two URL
variants all 404.

Five of the fourteen results are research-database entries for other conditions (Guillain-Barre,
pancreatitis, neurological, meningococcal, hearing loss) that matched only because EBV is named
inside a study abstract on each.

Not one source in the entire library contains: mononucleosis as an illness, viral latency,
dormancy, reactivation, antibody titers, EBV serology, or seroprevalence.

**Consequence: the caption is the primary source.** The parent-brand articles are framework and
voice donors only. Every EBV-specific claim on the page traces to the caption or to CDC.

## What carried over from the library

- **Prevalence defeats causation.** Nearly everyone carries EBV and nearly everyone is fine, so
  the virus was never the variable that changed. From the ADP Q&A terrain argument. This is the
  single best idea in the library and it is on the page as a pull quote. It also inoculates the
  page against reading like the "EBV causes everything" content it will rank beside.
- **The fireman found no fire.** A patient told nothing is wrong while still feeling terrible.
  Reworked into the "your labs came back fine" section without the fireman metaphor, which was
  already used on the PCOS page.
- **Autoimmunity as adaptation, not malfunction.** The immune system reorganizing around a trigger
  that never left. Informs the framing throughout.
- **The 3 T's**, mapped onto the caption's own trigger list.

## Citations

CDC is the only source cited on the page, covering the four load-bearing facts: over 90% of adults
carry antibodies, the virus persists for life after first infection, VCA plus EBNA indicates
infection from months to years earlier, and high antibody levels can persist for years and are not
on their own diagnostic of recent infection.

**The caption's titer claim was softened deliberately.** "Antibody titers tell you whether this is
an old infection or an active one" is stronger than CDC supports. The page instead says the panel
distinguishes a recent first infection from one years ago, and states plainly that a single
elevated number does not prove current activity. Ryan reviewed this and approved shipping.

## Excluded, and why

Every agent independently flagged the same categories.

1. **All vaccine content.** Present in six of the nine articles, including claims that vaccines
   cause autoimmunity, a post-vaccine immune panel showing damage, and innuendo tying two named
   deaths to vaccination. Outside DC scope, unsupported, and a platform risk. Zero on our page.
2. **All medication content.** Named drugs with brand names across nearly every article, usually
   paired with harm claims. Zero on our page, including in the disclaimer.
3. **Cancer and lymphoma claims**, including the assertion that immune-suppressing therapy may
   cause non-Hodgkin's lymphoma.
4. **Subluxation causing systemic or visceral disease.** Uncited everywhere it appears and the
   claim most specific to our own service.
5. **Supplement protocols.** Thirteen named supplements in the MS article alone, several citing
   rodent studies in the same register as human trials.
6. **Cure, reverse, resolve, and restore-fertility language.**

## Voice

Built from the caption's fingerprint rather than the parent brand's article register. Target was
bimodal sentence length with a cluster at three to six words and another at fourteen to twenty
two, roughly 40% fragments, second person throughout, technical terms stated bare and cashed out
in lived experience rather than defined, and no em dashes, semicolons, or colons.

Shipped page measures: 1642 words, mean sentence 10.2 words, 37% at six words or fewer, zero
dashes of any kind, zero semicolons.

The closer is the caption's three lines, near verbatim, at the end where they earn their place.
